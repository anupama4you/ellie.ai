The Real Cost of a Missed Lead for a Tradie

You're on the tools, halfway through a job, and your phone rings. You can't answer. The caller hangs up and, more often than not, rings the next tradie on the list. That's not just a missed call. That's a missed job, a missed referral, and potentially thousands of dollars walking out the door.

Most tradies know this happens. Very few have actually sat down and worked out what it costs them. This article helps you do exactly that.

Why Missed Calls Hit Trades Harder Than Most Industries

Unlike a retail shop or an office-based business, tradies are almost always physically occupied when a call comes in. You're under a sink, on a roof, or driving between sites. You can't exactly put down the angle grinder to take an enquiry.

The problem is that most people calling a tradie want a quick response. They have a leaking pipe, a busted air conditioner, or a bathroom reno they need quoted. If you don't answer, they move on. Industry research consistently shows that most callers who reach voicemail do not leave a message, and the majority who do leave one do not wait more than a few hours before calling a competitor.

This dynamic makes the trades sector particularly vulnerable to lost revenue from unanswered calls.

How to Calculate the Value of a Single Missed Lead

Let's make this concrete. Here's a simple framework any tradie can use to put a dollar figure on a missed call.

  • Average job value: What does a typical job earn you? For a plumber it might be $350 for a call-out, or $8,000 for a bathroom renovation. For an electrician, a switchboard upgrade could be $2,500. Pick your realistic average.
  • Close rate on answered enquiries: If you answer the phone and quote, how often do you win the job? Many tradies estimate 40 to 60 percent for inbound calls, since the person is already looking for someone.
  • Missed call rate: How many calls do you miss each week? Even two or three per week is common for a solo operator.

A basic calculation: if your average job is worth $1,200, your close rate on answered calls is 50 percent, and you miss five calls a week, you're potentially leaving $3,000 on the table every single week. That's over $150,000 a year in lost opportunity.

Obviously not every missed call was a genuine lead, and some people do call back. But even a conservative version of this maths should give you pause.

The Hidden Multiplier: Referrals and Repeat Work

The job value calculation above only tells half the story. The real cost of a missed lead includes what that customer might have been worth over time.

A homeowner who gets their hot water system replaced is also the person who'll need their kitchen renovated next year, their garden lighting wired up, or their fence fixed. They're also the person who recommends tradies to neighbours, family, and friends. In tight-knit Australian suburbs and regional communities, one good relationship can produce five or ten jobs over a few years.

When you miss that first call, you lose all of it. The lifetime value of a customer in the trades can easily be two to five times the value of the initial job.

After-Hours Calls Are the Most Expensive Ones to Miss

A significant chunk of trade enquiries come in outside business hours. Homeowners call in the evening after they get home from work. They call on Saturday morning when they finally have time to sort out that leaking tap. They call on public holidays when something goes wrong at the worst possible time.

These are often high-urgency calls where the customer needs someone fast and is willing to pay accordingly. If you're not answering, someone else is getting that job and that premium rate.

This is exactly the scenario we wrote about in detail in our article on weekend call answering for trades. The short version: the tradies winning the most work are the ones who are reachable when others aren't.

Voicemail Isn't the Answer

Some tradies rely on voicemail as a safety net, assuming callers will leave a message if the job is real. This is a reasonable assumption, but research doesn't support it. Most people prefer not to leave voicemails, particularly for enquiries where they're comparing multiple providers. They want a human response, or at least a way to get information and book in quickly.

What Tradies Are Doing to Fix This

The most common solutions tradies try are hiring an admin person, using a human answering service, or just accepting the missed calls as part of doing business. Each has its limits.

Hiring admin staff adds cost and complexity, and you still lose calls when they're at lunch or on another line. Human answering services can work, but they often can't answer specific questions about your services, your pricing, or your availability. They take a message and the caller still has to wait.
