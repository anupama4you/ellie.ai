24/7 Phone Answering for Small Business: What Does It Actually Cost?

If you run a small business in Australia, you already know the feeling: your phone rings while you're with a customer, on a job site, or asleep, and you have no way to answer it. The question isn't whether missed calls cost you money. They do. The real question is what it costs to fix the problem, and which option actually makes sense for your situation.

This article breaks down the main 24/7 phone answering options available to Australian small businesses, what each one genuinely costs, and what you give up or gain with each choice.

Why 24/7 Coverage Matters More Than It Used To

Customer expectations have shifted. People searching for a tradie, a clinic, or a salon on a Sunday evening expect someone, or something, to respond. Industry research consistently shows that most callers who reach voicemail do not leave a message and do not call back. They simply move on to the next result in Google.

For many small businesses, the after-hours window is where leads are actually being lost. A plumber who only answers calls from 8am to 5pm is handing potential jobs to competitors every single evening and weekend. The same goes for hair salons, physio clinics, cleaning companies, and most other service businesses.

If this sounds familiar, you're not alone. The good news is that the options for solving it are more varied and affordable than they were even a few years ago.

Option 1: Hire a Full-Time Receptionist

The most obvious solution is also the most expensive. A full-time receptionist in Australia earns between $55,000 and $70,000 per year in base salary, depending on your city and industry. Add superannuation, leave entitlements, and payroll overhead, and you're looking at a total employment cost closer to $75,000 to $90,000 annually.

And that still only gets you coverage during business hours, typically Monday to Friday. If you want genuine 24/7 coverage, you'd need multiple staff members across shifts, which makes the cost prohibitive for almost any small business.

A receptionist is a great choice if your call volume is high, your front-of-house needs are complex, and you can afford it. For most small operators, it's simply not a realistic option for round-the-clock coverage. If you want a more detailed comparison, this article on AI receptionists versus hiring staff is worth reading.

Option 2: Human Answering Services

Human answering services use a shared pool of operators who answer calls on behalf of multiple businesses. You pay for the service rather than a dedicated employee, which brings the cost down significantly.

In Australia, human answering services typically charge in one of two ways: a monthly retainer that includes a set number of calls or minutes, or a pay-per-call model. Monthly plans generally start around $80 to $150 for very basic coverage, but plans that include genuine 24/7 availability with meaningful call volume tend to sit between $300 and $800 per month or higher, depending on your usage.

The limitations are worth understanding. Operators are handling calls for dozens of businesses simultaneously, so they work from a script. They can take a message and pass it on, but they typically cannot answer specific questions about your services, check your actual availability, or book appointments directly into your calendar. Response times can also vary, and quality is inconsistent across providers.

Option 3: AI Phone Receptionists

AI receptionists are the newest category and the one that has changed the maths for a lot of small business owners. Unlike a human answering service, an AI receptionist can hold a full conversation, answer questions about your business, and book appointments automatically, at any hour, without you needing to be involved.

Pricing for AI receptionist services in Australia generally starts from around $199 per month. For that, you get genuine 24/7 availability with no per-call fees eating into your budget as volume grows. The cost stays predictable regardless of whether you get ten calls or a hundred in a week.

AI receptionists work especially well for businesses with consistent, repeatable call types: booking appointments, answering FAQs, capturing lead details, confirming hours and pricing. They are less suited to highly complex calls that require nuanced human judgement or emotional sensitivity, though they handle the majority of real-world small business calls without issue.

Option 4: Voicemail With a Callback System

Some business owners try to manage missed calls by setting up a professional voicemail and committing to rapid callbacks. This is essentially free to implement, but the cost shows up elsewhere. Most callers do not leave voicemails, and of those who do, a meaningful portion will have already called a competitor by the time you get back to them.

If you're in a competitive service category, a voicemail strategy is not a 24/7 solution. It's a delayed response that works in your favour only when customers have no other options. That is increasingly rare.

Comparing the Real Costs Side by Side

  • Full-time receptionist: $75,000 to $90,000 per year, business hours only
  • Human answering service (24/7): $300 to $800 per month or more, depending on call volume
  • AI receptionist: from $199 per month, unlimited availability
  • Voicemail: near zero upfront, ongoing cost in lost leads

For most small businesses, the AI receptionist category offers the best combination of genuine 24/7 coverage, consistent quality, and cost that doesn't scale aggressively with call volume.

Which Option Is Right for Your Business?

The honest answer depends on your call volume, the complexity of those calls, and your budget. Here are some rough guides:

  • If you receive high call volumes and calls require detailed human input, a part-time or full-time receptionist may be worth the cost.
  • If you want human operators for after-hours peace of mind and call volume is low, a human answering service could work.
  • If you want 24/7 coverage that books appointments and answers questions automatically without staff overhead, an AI receptionist is worth serious consideration.
  • If your calls are genuinely infrequent and low stakes, voicemail with fast callbacks may be enough for now.

Tradies, salons, clinics, and other appointment-based businesses in particular tend to get a lot of value from 24/7 AI coverage, since a large proportion of their calls are booking requests that don't require a human to resolve.
