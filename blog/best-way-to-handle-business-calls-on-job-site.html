The Best Way to Handle Business Calls When You're on a Job Site

You're halfway through a roof repair, hands full, power tools running. Your phone rings. You ignore it, finish the job, and call back an hour later. The customer has already booked someone else.

This is the daily reality for most Australian tradies, and it costs real money. Not just one job either. A caller who can't reach you often assumes you're unreliable, even if you're simply doing what you're paid to do: working.

So what's the actual best way to handle calls when you're physically on the tools? Let's go through the real options.

Why Voicemail Doesn't Cut It Anymore

Voicemail feels like a safety net, but industry research consistently shows that most callers who reach voicemail don't leave a message at all, and those who do rarely wait more than an hour for a callback before trying a competitor.

For a tradie running a busy schedule, an hour might as well be a week. By the time you're done on site, cleaned up, and returning calls, the opportunity is gone. Voicemail was designed for a slower era. It doesn't reflect how people book services in 2025.

Option 1: Answer Every Call Yourself

Some tradies try to answer every call personally. It sounds like good customer service, but in practice it creates problems:

  • You stop work constantly, breaking your focus and your workflow
  • Customers can hear the noise in the background and it seems unprofessional
  • On safety-critical jobs, stepping away from the work creates genuine risk
  • You end up fielding tyre-kickers and time-wasters mid-task

For a solo operator especially, trying to be your own receptionist while also being your own labourer is a recipe for doing both jobs poorly.

Option 2: Hire Someone to Answer Calls

A part-time admin person or receptionist seems like the logical next step. And for some larger trade businesses, it makes sense. But for a sole trader or a crew of two or three, the numbers rarely stack up.

You're looking at a minimum of around $25 to $35 per hour for a part-time admin worker in Australia, plus super, plus the time spent hiring and managing them. And they're only available during business hours, which means evenings and weekends, the times when homeowners actually call to book a tradesperson, still go unanswered.

If you want to compare the costs in more detail, the article on AI receptionist vs hiring a receptionist breaks it down clearly.

Option 3: Use a Call Answering Service

A call answering service uses real humans to pick up your calls on your behalf. They take a message, sometimes qualify the caller, and relay the information back to you. It's better than voicemail, but there are limitations worth understanding.

Most answering services charge per call or per minute, which adds up quickly if your phone rings often. The operators don't know your business intimately, so they can struggle with specific trade questions. And they can't actually book jobs into your calendar or send a confirmation to the customer.

What About After-Hours Calls?

This is where the gap between traditional options and AI becomes most obvious. Homeowners don't follow business hours. They search for a plumber at 9pm, find your website, and call. If no one answers, they move on to the next result.

Most call answering services either stop at 5pm or charge significantly more for after-hours coverage. An AI receptionist operates around the clock at no extra charge, which for tradies doing urgent or emergency work can be a genuine competitive advantage.

The Real Cost of Missing Calls

It's easy to think of a missed call as a minor inconvenience. But consider a tradie who misses two quote requests a week. If even one of those would have converted into a $600 job, that's around $2,400 a month walking out the door. Over a year, that's significant.

Practical Tips If You're Not Ready for a Full Solution

If you're not ready to commit to any paid option, here are some things that help in the short term:

  • Set up a professional voicemail that tells callers when you'll call back and gives them an alternative way to reach you, like SMS
  • Enable SMS auto-reply on your phone so missed calls get an instant text response
  • Use a booking link in your voicemail message so callers can self-book without waiting
  • Block time each morning and afternoon specifically for returning calls, and stick to it

These won't solve the problem entirely, but they reduce the damage of a missed call while you work out a longer-term approach.
