How to Stop Missing Calls at Your Salon

If you run a hair salon, beauty studio, or nail bar, you already know the feeling. You're in the middle of a colour treatment, your hands are full, and your phone rings. By the time you're free, there's no voicemail. The caller has moved on and booked somewhere else.

Missed calls are one of the most common and quietly expensive problems for salon owners in Australia. The good news is that the problem is entirely solvable, without hiring extra staff or chaining yourself to the front desk.

Why Salons Miss So Many Calls

Salons are hands-on environments. You cannot put down the foils to answer the phone. Your team is booked back-to-back, clients are in the chair, and the front desk is often unmanned during peak hours or between appointments.

Add to that the reality of trading hours. Many salons are open Tuesday to Saturday, closed Sunday and Monday. A potential client who calls on Sunday afternoon to book a Wednesday appointment will very likely call a competitor before Monday morning rolls around. Industry research suggests most callers who reach voicemail do not leave a message, and fewer still call back. That is a booking you will never know you lost.

The problem compounds during your busiest periods, which are often the exact times you can least afford to answer the phone: school holidays, the lead-up to Christmas, hen's party season, and wedding weekends.

The Real Cost of Unanswered Calls

A single missed booking might seem minor. But think about the average spend per visit at your salon, multiply it by the number of calls you miss each week, and then factor in the lifetime value of a repeat client. The numbers add up quickly.

There is also the reputational cost. Clients who cannot get through by phone may leave a Google review mentioning poor communication. New clients searching for a local salon often call several options and book with whoever answers first.

Option 1: Hire a Receptionist

The traditional solution is to employ someone at the front desk whose primary job is to answer the phone, handle bookings, and greet walk-ins. For larger salons, this makes sense. But for small and mid-sized salons, the economics are difficult.

A part-time receptionist in Australia will typically cost between $25 and $35 per hour, before you factor in superannuation, leave entitlements, and training time. That is a significant overhead for a business where margins are already tight. And they still cannot answer calls after hours or on their days off.

Option 2: Use a Call Answering Service

A human call answering service uses a team of remote receptionists to answer your calls on your behalf. They can take messages, answer basic questions, and sometimes book appointments depending on the service level.

These services typically charge per call or per minute, which can work out reasonably for low call volumes. However, they tend to be less effective for salons where callers want to check specific availability, ask about pricing for a particular service, or book for a group. The remote operator may not have the specific knowledge to handle those conversations well, and callers can sometimes tell they are not speaking with someone who actually knows the business.

What to Look for in a Solution for Your Salon

Whatever option you choose, these are the things that matter most for a salon environment:

  • 24/7 availability: Your clients want to book outside business hours. If your solution only works during the day, you are still missing calls.
  • Appointment booking: Taking a message is not the same as locking in a booking. You want a solution that can confirm the date and time on the spot.
  • SMS confirmations: Reduces no-shows. Clients remember their appointment when they have it in writing.
  • Familiarity with your services: The receptionist, human or AI, needs to know what you offer, how long each service takes, and any relevant policies like cancellation terms.
  • Quick setup: You do not want to spend weeks onboarding a new system. Look for something that can be running within a day or two.
