# Ellie Landing Page Repositioning Brief

## Purpose

Use this document as the strategic brief for rewriting and restructuring the **Ellie (callellie.com)** landing page.

The goal is to stop positioning Ellie as just another **“24/7 AI receptionist”** and instead position it as a **fully managed AI front desk that is customised around each business, completes real work, and demonstrates measurable business value.**

The landing page should make Australian business owners understand within seconds:

> **Ellie doesn't just answer your phone. She runs your front desk.**

---

# 1. Core Strategic Problem

The AI receptionist market is becoming crowded.

Competitors increasingly offer similar baseline features:

- 24/7 answering
- Natural AI voices
- Australian voices
- Appointment booking
- Call transfers
- SMS notifications
- Calendar integrations
- CRM integrations
- Call summaries
- Free trials

These features should still appear on the website, but **they should NOT be Ellie's primary differentiation.**

Do not build the landing page around generic claims such as:

- “Never miss another call”
- “24/7 AI receptionist”
- “AI-powered answering service”
- “Save time with AI”

They are useful supporting messages, but they are no longer enough to differentiate Ellie.

---

# 2. New Positioning

## Category

Position Ellie as a:

**Managed AI Front Desk for Australian Businesses**

rather than simply:

**AI Receptionist**

## Primary positioning statement

> **Your business doesn't adapt to Ellie. Ellie adapts to your business.**

## Strong alternative brand statement

> **Ellie doesn't just answer your phone. She runs your front desk.**

## Supporting positioning

Ellie is configured around the customer's actual:

- staff
- services
- business hours
- appointment rules
- locations/service areas
- escalation rules
- calendars
- workflows
- customer questions
- existing software

The business owner should not need to become an AI expert or configure complicated workflows themselves.

**We build their Ellie for them.**

# 3. The Four Brand Pillars

The landing page should communicate four major promises.

## 3.1 BUILT FOR YOU

**Not another generic AI receptionist.**

Ellie is customised around the way the business actually operates.

Possible copy:

> Your staff. Your services. Your hours. Your rules. Your Ellie.

Explain that onboarding can configure:

- staff members
- staff working hours
- services each employee provides
- appointment durations
- service areas
- preferred call handling
- FAQs
- escalation rules
- transfer rules
- booking rules
- existing software

Important message:

> **We set it up for you.**

Avoid making Ellie look like another DIY SaaS dashboard where customers need to configure everything themselves.

---

## 3.2 DOES THE JOB

Ellie should be positioned as an AI that **takes action**, not merely talks.

Possible headline:

> **More than answering. Ellie gets things done.**

Possible actions:

- Answer calls
- Qualify new leads
- Book appointments
- Reschedule appointments
- Cancel appointments
- Find staff availability
- Assign customers to available staff
- Transfer important calls
- Escalate urgent calls
- Send SMS confirmations
- Capture customer details
- Notify staff
- Update connected systems
- Create jobs/leads where integrations support it

The distinction should be clear:

**Competitor-style message:**

> AI answers your calls.

**Ellie message:**

> Ellie handles the enquiry and completes the next step.

---

## 3.3 GETS SMARTER

Position Ellie as an ongoing managed service rather than software that is installed and forgotten.

Possible headline:

> **Your receptionist should get better over time. Ellie does.**

Explain that businesses can continuously refine:

- call flows
- frequently asked questions
- escalation rules
- qualification questions
- booking behaviour
- responses
- workflows

Long-term product direction can include insights such as:

- questions customers repeatedly ask
- reasons calls are escalated
- common booking requests
- after-hours demand
- lead patterns
- areas where customers abandon calls

Do not claim automated capabilities that are not currently implemented. Present future/advanced functionality only when supported by the product.

---

## 3.4 SHOWS THE VALUE

Ellie should ultimately demonstrate business outcomes rather than vanity metrics.

Avoid making the dashboard story primarily:

> 127 calls answered

Prefer an outcome-oriented presentation such as:

> **This month Ellie handled:**
>
> 127 calls  
> 31 new enquiries  
> 18 appointments booked  
> 7 urgent calls escalated  
> 12 after-hours opportunities captured

Future functionality can include:

> **Estimated opportunity value: $8,450**

If estimated revenue/opportunity value is displayed, clearly identify it as an estimate rather than confirmed revenue.

Potential feature name:

**Opportunity Tracking**

or

**Revenue Opportunity Tracking**

# 6. Staff-Aware Booking

This should become a prominent product capability.

Suggested heading:

## One receptionist. Your whole team.

Explain that Ellie can be designed to understand:

- multiple staff members
- different working hours
- different services
- individual availability
- customer staff preferences
- “any available staff member” requests

Example interaction:

**Customer:**

> Can I get a haircut tomorrow afternoon? Anyone is fine.

**Ellie:**

> Absolutely. Emma has 2:30 or 3:30 available, and Sarah has 3:15. Which works best?

Then Ellie completes the booking against the appropriate staff member.

Also communicate:

**Book → Reschedule → Cancel → Reallocate**

Only advertise capabilities that are currently production-ready. If some are still being developed, structure the page so they can be enabled later without making misleading claims now.

Examples:

|---|---|
| Normal booking | Ellie handles it |

# 10. Integrations Strategy

Do not make the primary message:

> Integrates with hundreds of apps.

Prefer:

> **Works with the tools your business already uses.**

Prioritise **deep integrations** that allow Ellie to complete workflows rather than superficial logo collections.

Possible integration categories:

- Calendars
- Booking platforms
- CRM
- Job management
- SMS
- Business management software

Potential vertical integration roadmap includes systems such as Timely, ServiceM8, Tradify, Cliniko/Halaxy and other high-value Australian platforms, subject to API access and commercial/technical feasibility.

Do not display an integration as supported until it actually works reliably.

# 16. Recommended Landing Page Architecture

Restructure the page approximately as follows:

1. **Hero** — differentiated positioning + CTA
2. **Trust / credibility** — Australian positioning, setup included, etc.
3. **Why Ellie is different** — customised rather than generic
4. **How it works** — Tell us → We build → Ellie works
5. **Actions** — demonstrate what Ellie actually completes
6. **Staff-aware booking** — multi-person scheduling capability
7. **Smart Escalation** — demonstrate human + AI workflow
8. **Industry workflows** — show Ellie working differently by industry
9. **Integrations** — existing tools and workflows
10. **Business outcomes / ROI** — leads, bookings, opportunities
11. **Managed setup** — remove technical anxiety
12. **Demo / call Ellie** — scenario-based experience
13. **Testimonials/results** — measurable social proof
14. **Pricing/offer** — simple and transparent where appropriate
15. **FAQ** — objections
16. **Final CTA**

Do not make every section equally large. Maintain strong visual hierarchy and plenty of whitespace.

# 17. Objections the Page Must Answer

The copy should naturally answer these concerns.

## “Will it sound robotic?”

Demonstrate the voice instead of simply claiming it sounds human.

## “What if it gives customers the wrong information?”

Explain controlled business knowledge, configured workflows and testing.

Avoid promising perfect accuracy.

## “What happens if the AI can't handle something?”

Explain Smart Escalation and human handoff.

## “Will I have to configure all of this?”

No — managed setup is a key Ellie proposition.

## “Can it work with my staff?”

Explain staff-aware workflows and availability.

## “Can it use my existing systems?”

Explain supported integrations and custom integration possibilities without overstating compatibility.

## “Why shouldn't I just use voicemail?”

Focus on capturing intent and taking action while the customer is actively contacting the business.

## “Why Ellie instead of another AI receptionist?”

Answer with the complete positioning:

> Ellie is built around your business, configured for you, capable of completing workflows and designed to show the outcomes it creates.

# 21. What NOT to Do

Do not turn the landing page into a giant feature list.

Do not copy competitor language.

Do not lead entirely with “27% of calls go unanswered.” The statistic can still be used in ads and supporting sections, but the core brand should be bigger than a missed-call statistic.

Do not imply Ellie replaces every human receptionist.

Do not make unsupported claims about revenue, accuracy or integrations.

Do not advertise roadmap functionality as currently available.

Do not make the website overly technical.

Do not repeatedly say “AI.”

Do not compete primarily on price.

# 23. Instructions for Claude / Coding Agent

When implementing this brief:

1. First inspect the existing landing page structure, components, styling system and responsive behaviour.
2. Preserve the existing Ellie visual identity unless a change clearly improves conversion or readability.
3. Rework the information architecture around the positioning in this document rather than simply replacing individual sentences.
4. Prioritise the hero, differentiation, managed setup, Actions, booking and escalation sections.
5. Keep copy concise. Do not paste the entire strategic brief onto the website.
6. Use visual demonstrations, workflow diagrams, product UI and realistic call examples where possible instead of long paragraphs.
7. Maintain strong mobile responsiveness.
8. Keep CTA language consistent throughout the page.
9. Clearly distinguish currently available functionality from functionality that is not yet production-ready.
10. Do not invent customer numbers, testimonials, integrations, revenue figures, certifications or performance claims.
11. Reuse existing components where sensible, but create new components when required to communicate the new positioning effectively.
12. Keep the website feeling like a real Australian technology/service company rather than a generic AI startup template.
13. Before making major destructive structural changes, understand existing functionality and preserve working forms, analytics, demos and integrations.
